FADE IN:
1 EXT. NEIGHBORHOOD STREET - DAY
On a street full of potholes with small houses crowded too
closely, PRINCIPAL SEALES, a 51 year old White man, in a polo
with his school logo on the front, huddles with three of his
teachers. A leader amongst them, MS. CARTER, 35, addresses
them.
MS. CARTER
It's important for us to take this
first step. If we want the parents to
feel welcome and get involved, we need
to be the ones to open the door.
The teachers nod in agreement.
MS. CARTER (CONT'D)
A few things before we get started.
This is a very vulnerable community.
So just embrace, embrace, embrace!
PRINCIPAL SEALES
And if you notice people are
uncomfortable it may just be time to
explain in very clear terms why you're
on their doorstep.
MS. CARTER
Yes. And Principal Seales, we're so
grateful to have your guidance. I
think this could be the start of one
of our greatest school years.
The small group claps. He humbly accepts.
PRINCIPAL SEALES
I've entered a few schools in my day
that had students who needed a little
extra. It's taught me how much
advocating means to me and I hope you
all find that too. The most important
thing is to mean what you say to each
of these families today because this
is just the start.
An excited teacher emerges with cookies, handing them out.

2 EXT. FLORES FAMILY HOME - MOMENTS LATER
The front door of this small home opens to Seales who stands
with a smile and some cellophane wrapped cookies in hand.
MR. FLORES, 33, stands opposite him looking confused.
PRINCIPAL SEALES
Hello, I'm Principal Seales, the new
principal at Richland Elementary.
Mr. Flores immediately retreats, calls out for someone. He
holds a finger up to Seales who waits patiently with more
than ample time for awkward smiles. CELIA, an eight year old
girl approaches the door and stands in front of her father.
MR. FLORES
(in Spanish)
Continue.
CELIA
You can keep going.
SEALES
Well, hello. I'm your new principal.
What's your name?
She's silent. A bit shy.
CELIA
Celia.
SEALES
And what grade are you going to be in
this year, Celia?
CELIA
Second.
SEALES
Wow. I hear the second grade teachers
are the best ones! It's my first year
at Richland so maybe you can help me
get to know people.
She smiles. Mr. Flores taps her shoulder with a curious look.
CELIA
What can we do for you?

SEALES
I just wanted to introduce myself and
ask you and your parents if they have
any questions for me.
She turns to Mr. Flores and translates. As he watches, he
interjects nervously...
SEALES (CONT'D)
About the school...or me... Anything
really.
Beat.
CELIA
No.
Mr. Flores kindly nods as he begins to close the door. Seales
gently makes more space for himself.
SEALES
Celia, could you tell your father that
I want to know how I can get him
involved in our school?
She translates. Mr. Flores is hesitant, shifting his gaze
from her to Seales and back. She turns back to Seales.
CELIA
He says no one has ever asked him.
As Mr. Flores speaks in Spanish behind her, she translates.
CELIA (CONT'D)
We don't know if the other parents
would like us to be at the school. We
just want our daughter to get her
education while we work.
SEALES
Oh. Where do you work?
Mr. Flores is hesitant, shifting his gaze from her to Seales
and back before answering. Celia translates.
CELIA
At the food packaging plant.
SEALES
It sounds like you're an important
part of this community. I think the

other parents would be happy to get to
know you.
As she translates, he adds...
SEALES (CONT'D)
I would too along with our teachers.
Celia turns to him with the best adult posture she has.
CELIA
Sir. My father said they know what
people in this town feel about them.
He doesn't want to start trouble.
SEALES
Can you tell him that I'm not worried
about their immigration status? Unless
they need my assistance, I am not here
to ask questions. I'm only here to let
them know that EVERY child is welcome
at Richland including their family.
She translates and Mr. Flores give the most genuine smile
yet. Seales hands over the cookies to Celia.
SEALES (CONT'D)
They're monster cookies, my favorite.
Don't worry, I made sure to scare the
monsters away after they made them.
Celia smiles, clutching her cookie.
3 INT. RICHLAND ELEMENTARY, PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE - DAY
Principal Seales and Ms. Carter sit on either side of his
desk, dressed casually and hard at work. Ms. Carter lifts her
head excitedly from deep inside her laptop screen.
MS. CARTER
So I think this draft is clear and
concise! I have the data about the
amount of ELL students we have, their
particular needs, how much extra
funding we're requesting, why we think
it's a conservative...
PRINCIPAL SEALES
Send it to me. I'll give it a read.
Thank you, Trish.

MS. CARTER
No problem at all. I've wanted this
for our school for a long time and we
finally have the principal who's not
afraid to ask.
PRINCIPAL SEALES
Can't be afraid of the first step.
MS. CARTER
You could in this county. It's the
poster child for 'we like things to
stay the same.'
They laugh.
MS. CARTER
But really. It's been six years now
since we had that really sharp
increase of ELL students.
PRINCIPAL SEALES
Is that when the plant opened?
MS. CARTER
You got it. And all people can do is
complain that our achievement scores
dropped. But when we explain that we
need tools to help our ELL students
catch up, the complaints get louder.
There's no winning.
He sits back in his chair and gets serious.
PRINCIPAL SEALES
You really think asking for resources
for our ELL kids is gonna be hard?
She gives him a look as if to say "buckle up."
MS. CARTER
I mean, there's also the fact that
Giles Lacy's contract for
superintendent is up for renewal and
the school board supports him but it's
the county commissioners who he really
needs behind him.
He puts two and two together.

PRINCIPAL SEALES
(out loud to himself)
The very people we're asking the
superintendent to get the money from.
Her look says, "exactly."
4 INT. RICHLAND ELEMENTARY, HALLWAY - LATE AFTERNOON
Principal Seales finishes putting up a poster for the ELL
program, when a teacher approaches him with a grin.
TEACHER 1
Principal Seales. I just want to say
that the turn out for our open house
yesterday was the best I've seen in
years. I never thought I'd see the
community come out like that.
SEALES
Just gotta reach out.
(beat)
In fact, I got invited to speak at
event about our goals.
TEACHER 1
Oh yea? What event?
SEALES
It's a rotary club meeting.
Teacher 2 is caught off guard. He tries to make sense of it.
TEACHER 1
Well, I can't wait for them to hear
about our plans.
There's something foreboding but Seales is none the wiser.
5 INT. COMMUNITY CENTER - EVENING
Principal Seales enters backstage where he finds the only
familiar face he knows, CHARLES, 56, with whom he's been
communicating.
CHARLES
Principal Seales! Glad to have you.
We're just getting started.
They shake hands as there's loud applause from the crowd.

PRINCIPAL SEALES
Glad to be here. Someone's doing well.
CHARLES
Oh, we just introduced County
Commissioner Janson.
From behind the curtain Seales hears a bit of a speech.
JANSON (O.S.)
You elected me because I said I would
take this county back and that's just
what I intend to do.
Another eruption of applause. Seales is confused but when he
looks to Charles it's nothing but smiles.
CHARLES
So that's what I wanted to tell you.
Commissioner Janson and our president
will each give a speech and then
introduce you. If you need any time to
prepare feel free to use the space.
Seales nods in agreement and Charles exits. He takes a seat
and pulls notecards from his pocket.
ANGLE ON the note cards in Seales' hands. They read...
IT'S OUR DUTY TO ENSURE EQUITY IN OUR EDUCATION SYSTEM.
Seales' preparation is interrupted by applause.
JANSON (O.S.)
And with that I will pass it to your
president, Dr. Mark Henderson.
Applause from the crowd as Seales listens intently.
MARK (O.S.)
Thank you Commissioner. Janson was
right in that we cannot talk about
success of this community without
addressing those who have come to
wreak havoc and cause discord amongst
us.
Seales' suspicion peaks as he rises to his feet.
MARK (O.S.) (CONT'D)
Illegal immigration is an issue in

this country, thats for sure. But now
the invaders have come to our back
yard to take our good paying jobs.
Seales is shaken by what he's hearing. He begins to pace as
he hurriedly looks through his note cards. He searches for a
pen, uses the wall as his table and marks up his cards. Just
then Charles enters.
CHARLES
You ready? It sounds like he's just
about to introduce you.
Seales is almost too stunned to speak when he hears...
MARK (O.S.)
You can't imagine how happy I was to
hear that the district hired an expert
to be the new principal at Richland.
Charles nudges Seales to the entry of the stage where Seales
watches MARK, 47, continue on.
MARK
He's here to talk to you all about how
he intends to return the school to
greatness. Let's give a warm welcome
to Principal Christopher Seales.
Applause rings out. Mark looks at Seales in anticipation.
Seales tries to smile away his distressed impression.
6 INT. RICHLAND ELEMENTARY, PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE - DAY
Principal Seales sits at his desk working on his computer.
There's a knock on his open door and in walks Ms. Carter. She
takes a seat. Seales just stares at her. It's odd.
MS. CARTER
Did you forget about our meeting?
(off his silence)
About the curriculum?
SEALES
Oh, yes. Sorry.
MS. CARTER
You okay?
SEALES
Nothing to worry about. Just thinking

about my speech at the Rotary Club.
MS. CARTER
Ah, yes... I heard about that.
Seales looks surprised that Ms. Carter knows.
SEALES
I didn't know there would be other
speakers before me. Or that they'd be
spouting all their rhetoric whilst
introducing me. I was completely
blindsided.
MS. CARTER
I can only imagine. So what'd you say?
SEALES
Not much of anything. I had something
planned but stuck to basics. I didn't
even touch ELL. They looked kind of
disappointed when it was all over. I
rushed out as soon as I got off stage.
MS. CARTER
Don't let it get to you. Sounds like
you didn't say anything you didn't
want to.
SEALES
I'm pretty sure they don't like me
much but the fact that I was there
doesn't look good either. I've already
gotten an email from a disappointed
parent who said they didn't realize my
ideas aligned with Commissioner
Janson.
MS. CARTER
I think this can all be explained. How
about I let you get back to responding
to those emails? Just reassure them
it's not what they think. We can talk
about my curriculum tomorrow.
Seales nods in agreement as Ms. Carter leaves him typing away
on his computer.
7 INT. RICHLAND ELEMENTARY, MAIN OFFICE - LATER
Principal Seales emerges from his office with his bag and

keys in hand. Before he can leave, he notices Giles LACY, 46
and the superintendent, stand from a chair with a smile.
Giles reaches out to shake his hand.
GILES
Christopher. I'm sorry for coming so
late but it was the first chance I got
today. Do you have a few minutes?
SEALES
Of course. Let's head to my office.
8 INT. RICHLAND ELEMENTARY, PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE - CONTINUOUS
Seales closes the door and takes a seat across from Giles.
SEALES
So what do I owe the pleasure of
having the superintendent visit me?
They share a quick laugh.
GILES
I'll get right to it. Commissioner
Janson is going to ask you to support
his new policy on a business zoning
plan. Your support is very important
to him... and to me.
(off his silence)
I've read your petitions for funding
for ELL and I figured I should come
ask in person if I would have your
support.
Seales feels cornered and isn't sure what to do.
SEALES
For the zoning plan?
Giles nods.
SEALES (CONT'D)
I'd love to support it but if Janson
could find another way that wouldn't
displace the immigrant families
working at the factory, that would
make it more appealing to me. Those
students go to my school.

GILES
Our school. Look, I understand what
you're doing and I can appreciate it.
I'm all about equity and fairness.
Seales swallows his desire to respond to that one.
GILES (CONT'D)
I'm looking out for both of us.
Friction like this between a county
commissioner and a superintendent can
be detrimental.
SEALES
For you, Mr. Lacy.
Seales sighs.
SEALES (CONT'D)
The last thing I want is to cause you
any trouble, but that's why I'm asking
if there's any chance of finding
another way around this.
GILES
This isn't trouble. It's serious
ramifications for me at the next
contract vote.
Seales' suddenly feels the weight of this.
GILES (CONT'D)
This zoning plan is what won Janson
his seat and he will see it done. I
fully intend on giving my support and
you, as my hire, are part of that.
(beat)
It looks like you need some time to...
think about it. You're a smart guy. I
know you'll find solutions to any
problem that comes his way.
He stands and shakes Seales' hand.
GILES (CONT'D)
It's why I hired you.
Seales is left at his desk to contemplate.
