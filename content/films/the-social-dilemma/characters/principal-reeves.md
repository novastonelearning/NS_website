Principal Reeves is a 39-year-old woman who leads Freemont Elementary School with a genuine desire to do right by her staff, students, and community, but finds herself increasingly overwhelmed by the impossible task of managing social media crises that seem to multiply daily. She is motivated by a deep commitment to maintaining her school's reputation and supporting her teachers, yet she feels exhausted and defeated by the constant barrage of issues: teachers with public social media profiles, parents misconstruing Facebook statuses, Instagram accounts that violate district policy, and most troubling, an anonymous Twitter account (@anonymousdistrict) that posts insider information criticizing district leadership and policies. Her temperament is conscientious and anxious—she rubs her temples in defeat, looks on helplessly during crisis phone calls, and seeks reassurance from colleagues like Principal Lowe that she's "doing enough" in her role. Principal Reeves reacts to problems by trying to enforce district social media policies through teacher meetings and in-service trainings, but she feels like she's fighting a losing battle as new violations emerge constantly. When she discovers that Sarah Williamson, one of her own teachers, appears to be behind a critical tweet about district leadership, and then receives a screenshot from Principal Lowe showing that Janice Neely—a beloved paraprofessional she just had a warm conversation with—has posted a public Facebook accusation about bullying at Lincoln Middle School, Principal Reeves is left conflicted and in disbelief, standing in the superintendent's waiting room with the weight of these social media disasters pressing down on her just as she's about to enter a high-stakes meeting.
