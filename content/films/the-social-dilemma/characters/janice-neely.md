Janice Neely is a paraprofessional at Pikeville Elementary School and a single mother who works an hourly wage while raising her son Jacob (also called Jay), who has Asperger's syndrome and whose disability has begun to manifest more prominently at home, creating significant challenges for their family. She is motivated by an intense desire to provide the best possible education and support for her son, which is why she worked with Principal Sandrell to secure a transfer for Jacob to attend Lincoln Middle School instead of his zoned school—a decision driven by practical concerns about distance, time, and gas money that would be "crushing to an already tough situation." Janice's temperament is warm, dedicated, and deeply grateful—she is described as bringing an "infectious light" to her building every day and is "one of the hardest working people in the school," and she expresses profound appreciation to Principal Sandrell for including and supporting her through difficult years. However, when Jacob faces constant bullying at Lincoln Middle School with what she perceives as inadequate response from teachers and administration, Janice becomes increasingly upset and frustrated, reaching out to Principal Lowe (whom she calls Dr. Stillman in emails) multiple times seeking resolution and emphasizing how important it is for Jacob's experience at the school to be positive. She reacts to the escalating situation with growing distress, and when Jacob is suspended for defending himself against a known bully, Janice's frustration boils over into a public Facebook post accusing Principal Lowe and Lincoln Middle School of "looking the other way on bullying"—a dramatic shift from the patient, grateful paraprofessional who told Principal Reeves that "patience is the most invaluable thing I learned training for this work."
