Torian Jackson is a Black student at Mariana Adams High School with a lengthy disciplinary history that includes skipping school, fights, classroom disruptions, and paraphernalia possession, though he consistently maintains that these infractions were not his fault and that he was never the instigator. He is motivated by a sense of injustice and frustration, feeling targeted and misunderstood by school authorities, particularly School Resource Officer Frankie Green, whom his mother Trinity believes "has it out for" Torian. His temperament is defiant and resigned—when Officer Green's drug dog hits on his backpack during a random sweep and marijuana is discovered inside, Torian's response suggests both anger and defeat. Torian reacts to authority with visible discomfort, aware that his reputation precedes him and that adults like Frankie already view him with suspicion. His mother Trinity's mistrust of the school's approach—particularly the presence of police conducting "random sweeps" that feel like prison tactics in what she calls an "urban" school—reflects the broader context of Torian's experience, where he exists under constant surveillance and is immediately assumed guilty. When Torian ends up sitting in the front office waiting for his consequence under Principal Ford's newly announced zero tolerance policy, he represents the student most vulnerable to the harsh penalties Ford has promised, with no personal connections or social capital to potentially soften the blow of expulsion and criminal charges.
