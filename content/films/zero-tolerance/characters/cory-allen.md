Cory Allen is a 17-year-old Black student at Mariana Adams High School who is a model student with straight A's, a 32 on the ACT, volunteer tutoring experience, and a spotless disciplinary record—he has never been in trouble whatsoever. He is motivated by an intense desire to become the first person in his family to attend college, particularly to earn the trustee scholarship to Saint Duke's University, which would be a "life changing event that would put my family's generational trajectory on a different path." Cory's temperament is earnest, hardworking, and deeply grateful for the support he receives from Principal Ford, whom he views as a father figure and talks about constantly at home, even saying he wants to become a principal like Ford someday. He reacts to opportunities with determination and anxiety—he stayed up all night working on his college application essay and practiced obsessively for his scholarship interview, though he stumbled on the easiest question ("tell me about yourself"), which he laughs about with friends. Cory has an extraordinarily close relationship with Principal Ford, who treats him "like he's one of mine" (almost like his "second son"), writes recommendation letters, provides college advising, and selected him to serve on the prestigious Student Safety Council specifically to strengthen his college applications. His mother Davina is a single parent who works two jobs to make ends meet after Cory's father left when he was five, and she expresses profound gratitude to Ford for filling that void in her son's life. When Cory reaches into his backpack to pull out $10 to repay Ford for lunch money and a baggie of marijuana accidentally falls onto the ground in front of the principal, his world is turned upside down—his expressionless face and downcast eyes reveal the devastating realization that this single moment could destroy everything he and his mother have worked for, costing him his college scholarship, his clean record, and potentially his relationship with the man who has been like a father to him.
