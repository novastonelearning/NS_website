Mrs. Mary Johnson is a veteran teacher at Longhunter Elementary who has been awarded Teacher of the Year three times during her long tenure at the school and is widely regarded as a beloved, trusted member of the faculty with "tremendous support from all in the school community." She is motivated by a genuine desire to support her colleagues and help build a positive team environment, and she demonstrates remarkable empathy and wisdom in her interactions—comforting the gym teacher about Principal Flemming's safety concerns and gently helping him see the parents' perspective on Extreme Dodgeball injuries. Johnson's temperament is warm, solution-oriented, and deeply loyal; she understands the difficulty of following a long-tenured, beloved predecessor because she experienced it herself as a brand-new teacher who took over mid-year for a 27-year veteran, and she reaches out to Principal Flemming via email to offer her support, writing "I know what you are feeling right now, and I know that the past two years have been tough as you have tried to bring about some important and very needed changes." She reacts to Flemming's struggles by positioning herself as the potential "missing link" who could finally help him gain majority support for his initiatives and "separate Principal Flemming from Principal Allen," offering to be his sounding board and help him build the team he needs. However, despite her exemplary record and good intentions, the audit report reveals that Johnson held two small collections ($9 and $6) for five and six days respectively before depositing them—technical violations of the three-day policy that she, like all staff, was trained on and signed an acknowledgment form agreeing to follow. The stark difference between Johnson's minor infractions involving less than ten dollars held for a short period and her consistently supportive attitude versus Ms. Reid's egregious violations and pattern of resistance creates a painful dilemma for Flemming, who must decide whether to apply consequences uniformly or take into account the vastly different intentions, attitudes, and contributions of these two teachers.
