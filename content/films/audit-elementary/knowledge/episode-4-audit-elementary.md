FADE IN
1 EXT. SUNSHINE ELEMENTARY SCHOOL - MORNING ONE
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING, 46, a vanilla, rule-abiding man walks
excited through the front door and heads for his office. A
cheery little cornball. We follow him as he talks.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
Today is a potentially exciting day.
It's
(awkwardly)
Audit-Report-Receiving Day!
2 INT. FRONT OFFICE - CONTINUOUS - M1
He enters quietly.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(whispering)
Three years in and I haven't won the
people's hearts like the last guy did.
But maybe I've at least raised the
standards for this school.
ANGLE ON: DIANE, 72, school secretary, asleep in her chair.
3 INT. PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE - CONTINUOUS - M1
He hits the lights revealing that school paraphernalia threw
up inside. It's the office of a proud administrator. He
settles behind a "Principal Flemming" name plate at his desk.
ANGLE ON: Diane entering sluggishly with mail and
incontestable geriatric sass.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING (O.S.)
Mornin Sunshine! Anything you want to
say about my time here?
DIANE
Doesn't sound remotely interesting.
She hands over the mail, exits. He fingers through it.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
Mr. Allen has big shoes to fill.
(off the mail)
No audit report?

ADAM (O.S.)
(to himself)
Wow. Ridiculous.
ANGLE ON: ADAM, 38 the meticulous bookkeeper, as he reads
papers in an unlit corner of Flemmings office.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
Geez! Adam. Have you been there the
whole time?
ADAM
Listen Flem...
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
Don't call me that!
ADAM
(to camera)
The truth is the whole community loved
Mr. Allen. The most beloved principal
I've ever worked with. A ball of fun.
Flemming looks at the camera as if to sarcastically say
"thanks."
ADAM
(to camera)
Problem was the comptroller always had
a field day with him too. Too busy
people pleasing to pass an audit.
(beat) Me and Flemmy don't need people
to like us.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
Please stop.
ADAM
We may not be popular but we're
prepared.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(to camera)
I wouldn't say I'm unpopular...
MOTHER ONE TALKING HEAD
MOTHER ONE
I got two kiddos. I just gotta say
that if Principal Allen was still here
my first grader would never have this

much work. But I do gotta give credit
where credit's due. My boss did
recently comment on how my handwriting
is legible now.
She's pretty proud.
4 INT. PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE - MOMENT'S LATER - M1
Continuing with his thought.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
I mean, don't have a poll or anything
but I'd say me and Mr. Allen are neck
and neck on the likability scale.
He smiles confidently to camera.
GYM TEACHER TALKING HEAD
GYM TEACHER
I loved that guy. He never complained
when we played Extreme Dodgeball.
Flemsey gets here and all the sudden
it's a problem if one or a heap of 'em
get nose bleeds.
ANGLE ON: MRS. JOHNSON (more about her later) as she
approaches and puts a hand on his shoulder.
MRS. JOHNSON
(to Gym teacher)
If it makes you feel any better about
Principal Flemming, he's really trying
to save you from the wrath of parents
whose kids come home with bloody
shirts. really not fans of it They're
Extreme Dodgeball either.
He looks at her as if this is a thought he's never had.
5 INT. PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE - MOMENTS LATER - M1
Flemming continues...
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
Look, I'm not delusional. I know some
people aren't fans. I just like to
stay positive. What other choice do I
have?
(beat)

Honestly I think this school is great.
I love it and I'm proud to be the
principal of Sunshine Elementary. But
what kind of principal would I be if I
saw that things needed to change but
chose to keep it comfortable instead?
TEACHER MS. MAY TALKING HEAD
MS. MAY
Mr. Allen was really loved by this
community. In fact, he was my
principal when I went here. So I did
eventually achieve my dream of working
with him.
ANGLE ON: MS. REID (more about her later) approaching to hand
over a bulletin.
MS. REID
(while walking away)
It was really nice not being
micromanaged.
MS. MAY
Isn't that one of your students?
ANGLE ON: Window. Outside an 8 year old is selling candy from
her jacket on the playground.
MS. REID
Ugh. Do I really have to be on them
twenty-four/seven?
She exits.
MS. MAY
(to camera)
And don't get me wrong, it was laid
back and people were happy but the
students really needed the
improvements. I think despite the
challenges Mr. Flemming's done a lot
of good.
(beat)
RIP Mr. Allen.
DIRECTOR (O.S.)
Oh. Did he pass away?

MS. MAY
No.
(beat)
Retired.
She looks to camera as if to say "why would you think that?"
6 INT. PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE - MOMENTS LATER - M1
Flemming in deep thought.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(to camera)
When you love something you do the
hard stuff. Even if people don't like
you for it.
Adam continues looking through papers. Flemming talks at him.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(to Adam)
Hey! Remember those fun little emails?
Faculty loved . those
(off Adam's silence)
The ones about protocols? For the
audit? But in a cool way?
TEACHER TWO AND TEACHER THREE TALKING HEAD
TEACHER TWO
They were everyday.
TEACHER THREE
Every. Single. Day. One a day would be
a hideous underrepresentation.
(to Director)
Did he really say 'one?'
She rolls her eyes.
INSERT: TEACHER FOUR. Walks down empty hall. Frustration
about to boil over. DING! DING! DING! The sound of vibrations
is never ending. He whips his phone from his pocket, no
hesitation. Throws it in the trash as he passes.
7 INT. PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE - A LITTLE LATER - M1
Flemming pours himself some coffee.

PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(to camera)
Everyone was trained on the procedures
during July in-service and again after
winter break. They signed an agreement
stating that they understood the
procedures and that there would be
consequences for violating them. We
were thorough. So it's not too crazy
to expect perfection, right?
He's too positive. Adam cuts right through it...
ADAM
Wrong. Perfect audits don't exist. You
get docked for using the wrong color
ink.
ANGLE ON: Flemming as he tries to discreetly hide his mug of
colorful pens.
ADAM (CONT'D)
Plus there's these three findings.
Flemming is confused about what Adam is referencing.
ADAM
Oh. Did I not tell you? I'm looking at
the audit report.
ANGLE ON: Flemming immediately looking "fed up."
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(to the camera)
'Findings' aren't as gentle as they
sound. Auditors want to be told every
detail with meticulous documentation.
You never want them to 'find'
anything.
He looks to Adam who is still deep into the report.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
You just had it the whole time?
Adam's not even hearing Flemming speak to him.
ADAM
(off the report)
Oh yea that's not good.

ANGLE ON: Flemming closes his eyes and speaks his mantra.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(to himself)
I only have control over the present.
ADAM
(to camera)
His radical positivity thing.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(correcting him)
Acceptance.
ADAM
(reads from report)
Perfect! Cause here's something to
accept. The Student Activity Fund had
a deficit in the unassigned fund
balance.
ANGLE ON: Flemming as he eyes the camera, surprised.
ADAM
It's nearly $1200.
ANGLE ON: Surprise turning to shock. Fleming's jaw drops.
ADAM (CONT'D)
The majority of which was mishandled
by none other than Ms. Sunshine
herself.
Trying to be fair. He picks his jaw up from the floor and put
his professional face back on.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(to camera)
Ms. Reid is head of the Sunshine
Committee.
ADAM
Which is hilarious and ironic.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
(to Adam)
That may be but let's not presume to
know the circumstances of her actions.
He can't hold it any longer. Filled with anticipation,
Flemming snatches the report from Adam.

PRINCIPAL FLEMMING (CONT'D)
How much was her doing?
He flips to another page, clearly searching for something.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING (CONT'D)
(off report)
Doesn't look great. Ms. Reid is
documented as holding some large sums
collected for the Sunshine Committee
for as long as thirty days after the
fact.
ADAM
We can't presume it was intentional
but we can call it
(singing)
. suspicious
Adam immediately regrets singing it.
ADAM (CONT'D)
Regardless, its repetitive. It's large
sums. It's something she agreed to
understanding. It's all the things!
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
True! Very true. I just need to audit
my emotions here...
He looks to the camera as if to say "did you catch that?"
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING (CONT'D)
Just have to make sure that I'm not
making decisions based on how I feel
about her past indiscretions. It's not
about deep personal wounds...
INSERT: A chaotic classroom where students run amuck. At her
desk, reading is Ms. Reid who gives an obviously fake smile
and thumbs up to Flemming who watches through the window.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING (CONT'D)
(V.O. as we see INSERT)
...from years of passive aggression by
the kind of person that hates joy and
chips away at your very being.
ANGLE ON: Adam who looks to Flemming as if to say "pitiful."

PRINCIPAL FLEMMING (CONT'D)
(excitedly to Adam)
The simple fact is she broke the rules
of the audit.
ADAM
It's policy.
(excitedly to Flemming)
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
And the great thing about policy is
that you never have to chose sides.
There are no sides. Only policies.
He starts to feel relief, when Adam realizes...
ADAM
Yea but the consequences aren't a part
of the policy.
(to the camera)
That's up to his discretion.
Adam cringes for Flemming who deflates into his chair.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
There's everything from a slap on the
wrist to relieving her of her
position.
(beat)
But I can assure you, they're
expecting nothing. Because that's what
Mr. Allen would have done.
DIRECTOR (O.S.)
But as you very subtly hinted, you've
had other issues with this teacher
right?
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
Ms. Reid? Uh. Yea! You've been with me
since I started here. I'm surprised
you never picked up on that. It's not
just audit. She's never allowed a
single change at this school come
easily.
8 INT. MRS. REID'S HOME OFFICE - DAY
FOCUSED ON THE SCREEN OF A LAPTOP is MS. REID, 33 overly-
confident, disingenuous and always ready to complain. She
listens as she is one of many in a faculty meeting being held

over Zoom.
In his typical cheery fashion, Principal Flemming addresses
his faculty who have, "this could have been an e-mail" faces.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING (CONT'D)
Khan Academy and videos of the like
just don't meet our standards. So no
more. Not even on those particularly
tough days. Videos can be teaching
tools. They cannot be teachers...
Alright, let's head into our break-out
rooms to come up with some other
solutions to supplement. Go team, go!
Now in the break-out room with Ms. Reid, Gym Teacher, Teacher
Two and MRS. JOHNSON, 54. She's eccentric, gentle and seems
to solve any problem in front of her with effortless wisdom.
MRS. REID
Okay I'll be quick so we can get off
of this call as soon as possible. If
we can't use videos to supplement, how
about, on the days we feel like we're
drowning... which I already know what
you're gonna say... we select
(using air quotes)
mentors.
TEACHER TWO
Use the students who are excelling to
teach the ones who are struggling?
MRS. REID
Exactly. I mean if we're already so
overwhelmed we feel like we need a day
of videos just to catch up, then we
obviously need help. So why not the
students who already have the material
down? They'll be learning by teaching
so there's something in it for them.
Mrs. Johnson and the others are horrified by the idea.
MRS. JOHNSON TALKING HEAD
MRS. JOHNSON
I've been doing this a long while.
Sometimes people can find a way to do
the thing they said was impossible if
you just let them know you think

they're heroic for tackling it.
Back in the break-out "room," Mrs. Johnson takes the lead.
MRS. JOHNSON
This is just a never-ending job,
right? Basically impossible. After
twenty-something years I think I've
finally gotten into a rhythm. Maybe
myself and some others could offer up
our free period to come assist when
you need it. I mean it would take a
superhero to do it all alone.
MRS. JOHNSON TALKING HEAD CONT'D
She winks at the camera.
Back in the break-out "room" there's almost a choreographed
pause as everyone awaits Mrs. Reid's response.
MRS. REID
We of course appreciate you Karen but
you shouldn't have to do that.
MRS. JOHNSON
Have you ever heard the saying, "alone
we can do so little; together we can
do so much."
MRS. REID
Come on. You have to be deaf, dumb and
blind to think that would work here.
TEACHER TWO
Well, the quote is from Helen
Keller... so...
Silence as everyone stews in the awkward moment.
MRS. JOHNSON TALKING HEAD CONT'D
MRS. JOHNSON
I mean teaching a really hard job. is
It kinda take a super hero. Have does
you ever seen that episode where
Superman doesn't save the day because
he's busy complaining about his other
responsibility? Yea me either.
Just then Mrs. Reid approaches.

MS. REID
(to Mrs. Johnson)
Thanks again Karen. Do you think I
could get some help this Tuesday...
and Wednesday... and Thursday.
Mrs. Johnson smiles ironically as if to say "glad to help."
9 INT. PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE - MOMENTS LATER - M1
His chin resting on his hands, Flemming thinks hard on this.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
It's not about the person. It's about
a broken policy.
ADAM
So you've made a decision?
ANGLE ON: Flemming's genuine stress and indecision.
ADAM (O.S.)
Let's move on to the next finding.
Okay. See here we have a case of
someone holding money again. Under ten
dollars. Four days max.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
A rule's a rule and in this case a
rule everyone said they knew would
have consequences for breaking.
ADAM (O.S.)
Have fun telling everyone that you
decided to fire MRS. JOHNSON.
Principal Flemming's head drops onto his desk like an
exhausted toddler.
ADAM (CONT'D)
Don't worry, at most this is an
inattentive mistake.
Adam realizes the director is lost in the contradiction.
ADAM (CONT'D)
(to camera)
I know they sound the same but they're
not. One is what looks like the
equivalent of car insurance not being
turned in for a month versus a couple

of dollars forgotten in a desk a few
days too long.
(beat)
More importantly, the people couldn't
be farther from the same. MRS. JOHNSON
is a staple in this community. She's
cherished and for good reason. It's
almost like if the school had a
grandmother.
(beat)
Oh please don't use that. It's like if
the school had a cool aunt.
ANGLE ON: Flemming as he lifts his head. Adam relieved his
turn is over, takes a seat.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
A rule is a rule. But also, there has
to be an explanation.
ADAM
What if Ms. Reid has one too?
Flemming's hit hard but pulls it together. He thinks aloud.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
Right. If I take two different
disciplinary routes it will surely
cause issues.
(beat)
But like you said the people accepting
the discipline are not at all the
same.
ADAM
On one hand, there's someone who
consistently stands in the way of the
school's progress. Just so happens she
made a bad choice. Clear cut.
PRINCIPAL FLEMMING
And I'm more than fine with taking
disciplinary action. But on the other
hand is someone I just don't believe
deserves the same.
ADAM
Look, I've never done your job but
from my point of view it's not
unreasonable to choose the discipline
based on the degree of the offense.

Just sayin...
Adam heads for the exit.
ADAM (CONT'D)
So lucky I'm not you.

MRS. JOHNSON
