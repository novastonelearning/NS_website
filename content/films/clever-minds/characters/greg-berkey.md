Mr. Berkey is a veteran history teacher at Richland Hills High School in his 50s—tall, scruffy-faced, and balding—who is fiercely protective of his professional autonomy and teaching methods that have served him well over decades in the classroom. He is motivated by a deep frustration with the Clever Minds curriculum, which he views as an oppressive constraint that prevents him from doing his job effectively, particularly when it comes to explaining historical context and sensitive topics without fear of offending someone. His temperament is volatile and confrontational; when pushed to his breaking point, his anger and frustration become evident as he openly declares his opposition to the curriculum. Mr. Berkey feels that so much of teaching history is explaining the past, and the new curriculum's restrictions make it nearly impossible for him to provide the nuanced instruction his students need. He reacts to the mandated change with outright defiance and anger, dismissing concerns about "potentially" offending someone as misguided. His resistance is not just philosophical—it's personal and emotional, rooted in years of experience and a belief that his expertise is being undermined by bureaucratic overreach that prioritizes political correctness over genuine education.
