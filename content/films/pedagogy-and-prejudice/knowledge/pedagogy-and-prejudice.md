Pedagogy and Prejudice
Revision 1
Austin Mitchell

1 INT. CLASSROOM - DAY
MS. WOODS (white, female, 20s) stands in front of a
whiteboard covered in mathematical scrawling. She waves her
arms enthusiastically, ushering her captive audience forward.
MS. WOODS
So you can see there's nothing
derivative about derivatives.
She beams at the students who roll their eyes. She scans the
crowd, trying to see if the blank stares are from their poor
taste in comedy or struggling with the lesson.
Ms. Woods' eyes stop at the back of the class. JAMAL (black,
male, teens) scrolls through Instagram, brazenly tuned out of
the lesson.
Ms. Woods shifts gears, her posture changing from the relaxed
slouch of the 'friend' teacher into a matronly authoritarian
with a spine a nun would envy.
JAMAL'S POV
His view bobs to the BEAT of 90s HIP HOP blaring in his
headphones.
The infinite scroll of dogs and memes darkens as a shadow
falls across his screen.
The bob stops.
His steadied gaze rises to meet the icy glare of Ms. Woods.
BACK TO SCENE
MS. WOODS
Jamal, are you watching a mesmerizing
math influencer, or are you choosing
to disrespect me and the rest of your
classmates?
JAMAL
(pullling out airpod)
Huh?
MS. WOODS
Give me your phone.
JAMAL
What, why? I aint bothering nobody.

MS. WOODS
You're bothering everyone around you
with this distraction. You may not
care about your education but your
peers do.
Jamal looks to the students around him. They all pretend not
to watch the confrontation, quickly looking away from him and
to the ceiling.
JAMAL
Seems like everyone's 'bout as bored
of your crappy lesson as I am.
MS. WOODS
Excuse me?
JAMAL
You're excused.
MS. WOODS
Jamal I am not here for your attitude.
Give me your phone now, with no more
lip, and maybe you'll get it back
after class.
JAMAL
Or what? Nothing. How 'bout you walk
yourself back up in front of that
whiteboard and get back to your
circles and crap before you embarrass
yourself any more.
In a blur Ms. Woods snatches the phone out of Jamal's hand.
MS. WOODS
(forced calm)
You'll be able to get this from the
office at the end of the day if you
bring an apology and a new attitude.
Ms. Woods walks back to her desk to lock the phone away. Her
HEART BEATS loudly in her ears.
A heavy MATH TEXTBOOK sails from out of frame, CRASHING into
the whiteboard, barely missing her head.
She ducks reflexively, a second too late.
MS. WOODS
*scream*

Ms. Woods turns to face Jamal, who now stands behind his desk
fuming.
His eyes glisten and his chest heaves.
JAMAL
I aint done nothing to you man. Give
me my phone back.
MS. WOODS
GO TO THE OFFICE! DAVID WILL BRING YOU
YOUR REFERRAL.
JAMAL
Man, forget you.
Jamal storms out of the classroom.
2 INT./EXT. VICE PRINCIPLE'S OFFICE - MOMENTS LATER
Jamal sits framed by a narrow window in a tiny plastic chair
with his arms crossed staring straight ahead at no one.
Half audible voices murmur indistinctly.
The reflection of VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN (male, black, 50s)
covers Jamal's image.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
(into phone)
...I understand Ms. Woods. I'll talk
with him....I'll make sure he
understands. Try to get through the
rest of the day, we'll get it sorted
out.
Vice Principal Milikan sets the phone into its cradle with a
sigh.
He goes back to the door and watches Jamal.
From the outside we see Vice Principal Milikan framed in his
door, his face unreadable.
Beat.
3 INT. VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN'S OFFICE - MOMENTS LATER
Jamal sits hunched chair across from Vice Principal Milikan.
Jamal stares at his feet.

VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
This is the third time in as many days
you've been in my office Jamal. I'm
starting to think we should just go
ahead and have your homeroom changed
to here.
Jamal doesn't smile. His face remains fixed in its mask of
irritated dismissal.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
C'mon Jamal what's going on?
JAMAL
Nothing's going on man--
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Nothing? Monday you were in here for
mouthing off to Coach Sharps, Tuesday
Mr. Gates sends you to me for refusing
to do you work, and now Ms. Woods
tells me you attacked her.
JAMAL
I didn't touch her! Man, this is some
bull...I wasn't doing nothing.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
So you weren't playing on your phone?
JAMAL
So what if I was? I was minding my own
business, she didn't even notice until
her stupid lesson was done. She's just
upset cause no one understood none of
that crap she was saying.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Then she was lying when she said you
threw a book at her?
JAMAL
I threw a book at the wall, man. It
wasn't nowhere near her.
They sit for a long moment and think.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Jamal, I'm gonna have to write you up
for this.

JAMAL
What?!
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
C'mon Jamal you know you can't be
acting like this--
JAMAL
Like what?
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Jamal, something's going on that's got
you upset. You need to talk to me, or
to your counselor, but don't take it
out on your teachers. Cause if you do,
then I got to write you up.
Jamal shakes his head, looks out the window.
JAMAL
Whatever man, just get me out of this
place.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
(sighs)
We'll do two days of in school,
starting tomorrow. I don't want to
have another referral from you, ok?
You're too smart to let yourself get
messed up by whatever this is.
4 INT. HALLWAY - AFTER SCHOOL
Vice Principal Milikan stands in the hall outside his office,
jacket and briefcase in hand, exhausted. He's almost home
free.
Ms. Woods rushes in from out of frame. The strain of the day
is obvious on her face.
MS. WOODS
Is it true? Did you really just slap
him on the wrist with two days of
I.S.S. after what he did?
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Two days of in school isn't a slap on
the wrist--

MS. WOODS
It isn't? He threw a book at my head,
Arthur. He could have ACTUALLY HURT
me.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Ashley, somethings going on with
Jamal. He needs us to be the adults in
the room and be unreasonably
reasonable until he's ready to talk
about it. Getting upset in front of
him or throwing him in suspension for
the rest of the year isn't going to
help him.
MS. WOODS
I can't believe this. A student
attacks me and you're upset that I got
upset in front of him?
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Ashley I didn't say that--
Ms. Woods storms off.
Vice Principal Milikan takes a half step towards her, thinks
better of it, turns to leave.
5 INT./EXT. VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN'S OFFICE- THE NEXT DAY
Vice Principal Milikan strolls into his office bright and
early.
He waves to the secretary in the office with his free hand,
his other is occupied carrying his briefcase, coat, and a
thermos.
The exhaustion from the previous day is absent, he has a
bounce in his step as he enters his office proper.
His good moods falters as he sees Ms. Woods and PRINCIPAL
WENTZ (60s) sitting in the two chairs in front of his desk.
He takes his time hanging his jacket, placing his briefcase
on a side cabinet, and resting his thermos on his desk.
Vice Principal Milikan eases himself into his chair,

surrendering his morning to the reckoning waiting for him.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Something witty here = quippy one
liner
PRINCIPAL WENTZ
Ms. Woods is concerned that you're not
prioritizing her safety.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
(to Ms. Woods)
I see. Well, I certainly didn't mean
to give you that impression in our
conversation yesterday.
MS. WOODS
That impression? Mr. Milikan, the only
thing you had to say when I came to
you, still obviously shaken up, was
that we 'have to be the adults in the
room.'
PRINCIPAL WENTZ
Certainly you can see how that might
come across as dismissive, Mr.
Milikan.
Vice Principal Milikan collects himself and sits even
straighter in his chair.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Yes...I certainly can. I apologize Ms.
Woods if you feel I don't take your
concerns seriously because I do.
MS. WOODS
(relieved)
It's alright. I think we were both on
edge after yesterday.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Yes...Well if that's all--
PRINCIPAL WENTZ
I'm afraid it isn't. You see I
reviewed Mr. Harris' file this morning
and I see that he's become something
of a repeat offender. Three referrals
just this week as a matter of fact.

VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Yes, that's why I thought in school
was the best course--
MS. WOODS
We think it's best if Jamal is in
O.S.S.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
We?
MS. WOODS
His teachers. The ones he picks fights
with everyday.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
I.S.S is appropriate.
MS. WOODS
We don't feel safe Arthur.
PRINCIPAL WENTZ
Climate Surveys back this up. Teachers
have been feeling less safe in their
classrooms over the past few weeks. It
directly coincides with the day of
Jamal's first referral.
Beat.
PRINCIPAL WENTZ
I have decided that for the safety of
our staff, and Jamal's peers, and for
the integrity of our community's
learning environment, it would be best
to...adjust Jamal's discipline to out
of school suspension. You understand,
of course.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
O.S.S.?
Beat.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Yes, that is...appropriate. I
appreciate you stepping in so swiftly.
5A INT. VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN'S OFFICE - DAY

6 INT. CLASSROOM - AFTER SCHOOL
Vice Principal Milikan enters to find Ms. Woods sitting
behind her desk grading a stack of papers that make a
phonebook look svelte.
IRIS by THE GOO GOO DOLLS BLARES from a small RADIO.
Mr. Milikan KNOCKS loudly on her OPEN DOOR.
She jumps in her chair, rests a hand on her chest to steady
herself, flicks the radio off, and flashes her bright smile
to him.
MS. WOODS
Do you need something, Arthur?
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
I just got off the phone with Mrs.
Harris.
MS. WOODS
Mrs. Harris?
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Jamal's Mother.
He walks toward Jamal's desk with slow deliberate steps.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
She was upset at having to miss work
to come pick up her son after I called
her not twelve hours ago and said he
was going to remain in school for the
next two days.
MS. WOODS
Ah, well I'm sure she understands.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
I'm afraid she doesn't. You see,
Ashley, Mrs. Harris believes that the
best place for her son isn't at home
right now. She sees that her son is
upset, that her son is hurting, that
her son needs a community of his peers
and mentors right now. She understands
that if her son is going to learn and
grow and achieve his full potential he
needs to be allowed to remain within
an environment that inspires him to do

all of those wonderful things. Being
removed from that environment doesn't
help him. Instead it takes away all
those influences her son needs most,
and puts him back in a neighborhood
full of people who only want to tear
him down.
MS. WOODS
Eloquent for an angry parent.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
She showed remarkable grace under the
circumstances.
Vice Principal Milikan begins pacing around the classroom,
examining the student made posters of graphs and math
theorems.
MS. WOODS
I'm sure you relayed Principal Wentz's
concern for the safety of our Faculty
and student body, though.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Oooh yes. She was quick to voice her
skepticism, however. After all Jamal
is still very much a child, grown as
he may be, and children do act out.
MS. WOODS
Attacking a teacher isn't acting out,
Arthur.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
No, it most certainly is not, and I
explained that to Mrs. Harris. She
remained...unconvinced.
MS. WOODS
Did she?
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
She did. After all, she couldn't help
wonder about our white students.
Wonder if they would raise these same
concerns. Wonder if they might receive
the same punishment.
Ms. Woods stands rigid, her eyes following Vice Principal
Milikan around the room.

Her face goes pale.
MS. WOODS
Arthur are you calling me racist?
Vice Principal Milikan meets her eyes with a flat look.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Ashley you and I both know you're not.
Let's not kid though, we've seen white
students cause more trouble than Jamal
without getting a single referral. A
white student back talks and they're
'engaged.' If they don't pay attention
it's because they're 'too smart' for
the class.
MS. WOODS
I would have sent any of my students
to your office if they did what Jamal
did.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
I absolutely, emphatically, believe
you. But would you have made Principal
Wentz remove them from school? Would
you have felt so afraid if it was a
small white girl instead of a tall
black boy?
Ms. Woods stammers for a moment, looks around flustered.
Vice Principal Milikan holds her gaze.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
We failed that boy, Ashley. I'm sorry
that he attacked you but we HAVE to be
better than this. We can't take kids
out of school when they act out.
That's when we need to love them the
most.
MS. WOODS
I'm not going to apologize for being
afraid.
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
I don't want you to. I just want you
to trust that when I'm prioritizing

the students it's not because I don't
care about my people.
MS. WOODS
These kids are always disrupting
class. You don't get it. When was the
last time you ACTUALLY TAUGHT a class,
Arthur?
VICE PRINCIPAL MILIKAN
Ashley it's my duty as an
administrator to recognize when you're
too close to something to see it. This
is it. We have to trust each other.
These kids can't afford for us not to.
Vice Principal Milikan walks towards the camera.
7 EXT. SCHOOL - PARKING LOT - MINUTES LATER
Vice Principal Milikan walks towards his car. His shoulders
slouched with exhaustion.
Principal Wentz waves from the driver's side door of their
luxury car, oblivious of the day they inflicted onto Vice
Principal Milikan.
Vice Principal Milikan pauses next to his car.
We see the wrestling match in his head playing out across his
face.
Does he get in and drive or tell Principal Wentz what he told
Ms. Woods?
